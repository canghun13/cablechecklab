Sources & method

HDMI routing claims need explicit boundaries.

The tools classify topology and reason from declared capabilities. They do not certify an exact product or observe a live link.

What the tools can establish

ResultMeaning
Topology classThe source/display behavior maps to a direct link, switch, splitter, matrix, or multiviewer.
Capacity requirementEntered sources, display zones, audio-only outputs, and spare ports fit a minimum input × output count.
Declared mode fitThe selected EDID policy and declared scaler behavior are consistent with the entered maximum modes.
Audio route requirementThe connector and transport class required by the requested audio route is identified.
Implicated layerControlled observations narrow the next test; they do not identify a defective chipset or cable.

What remains product-specific

Exact EDID tables, scaler behavior, supported timings, chroma and bit-depth conversion, HDR and VRR pass-through, CEC routing, ARC/eARC port assignment, audio codec handling, HDCP authentication, extender limits, switching time, firmware behavior, and cable performance must be verified for the exact model and installation.

Primary and public sources

  • HDMI Licensing Administrator — Ultra HDMI cablesOfficial cable names, bandwidth classes, certification labels, backward compatibility, and the explicit absence of a universal cable-length value.
  • HDMI Licensing Administrator — Enhanced Audio Return ChanneleARC purpose, supported high-bitrate audio classes, and cable guidance.
  • Dolby Professional Support — endpoint device testingPublic topology guidance for sources, TVs, receivers, EDID capability changes, audio formats, and A/V sync considerations.
  • Extron — EDID ManagerEvidence that EDID is managed across HDMI, DisplayPort, DVI, and VGA paths; cited as a concept and competition boundary, not a product recommendation.
  • Pulse-Eight — matrix no-signal troubleshootingPublic one-zone versus all-zone fault scope and direct cable/path checks.

Conservative rules used here

  • Lowest-common-mode is the safe shared-mode assumption when no per-output processing is declared.
  • Copied or fixed EDID can advertise a mode a weaker display cannot accept.
  • “Downscaler” proves only the exact conversions named by the manufacturer.
  • eARC transport capacity does not prove that every connected device passes or decodes a requested codec.
  • No result offers HDCP bypass, stripping, or circumvention guidance.
  • Unknown stays unknown; a version number or HDMI connector does not imply every optional feature.
