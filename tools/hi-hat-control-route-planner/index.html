    Strike and position are different information

    A module can hear a hi-hat hit yet never receive the foot position. An analog setup commonly needs both a strike path and a controller path; the module combines those observations. A supported integrated digital assembly may carry both through its exact digital connection. That does not make ordinary USB, MIDI or an arbitrary audio lead a compatible pad connection.

    Read the function, not just the label

    A binary switch does not promise continuous intermediate openings. A continuous controller also needs a documented receiving mode and compatible lead. Do not reject or accept an input solely because its faceplate says TRIG or CONTROL: particular manuals can assign controller functions differently.

    Example: analog strikes working, a continuous controller, but only a switch-state receiving function cannot meet a continuous-opening request. A documented analog strike path plus a supported separate continuous controller route can satisfy the declared requirement; physical range/calibration remains an exact-model task.

    Do not cross-connect to experiment

    Some controller inputs supply power. A strike pad must not be plugged into such an input merely because the connector fits. Follow the exact pad/controller/module manual and approved lead; this tool supplies no resistance, voltage, polarity or calibration recipe.
