Charging guide

Matching watts is necessary. Matching profiles is the real work.

USB Power Delivery is a negotiation between source and sink. A cable and every intermediate device add their own current and protocol boundaries.

Fixed profiles and programmable ranges

A fixed Power Data Object offers a specific voltage with a current ceiling. A Programmable Power Supply APDO offers a voltage range and current ceiling, allowing the device to request smaller voltage steps. “PPS supported” is incomplete unless the charger’s actual range covers the device request.

Never combine rows. Voltage from one APDO and current from another do not form a valid operating point.

The current boundary

Power is voltage × current. A normal USB-C current path covers up to 3 A. More than 3 A requires a 5 A electronically marked C-to-C cable. Current certified cable branding uses 60 W and 240 W power markings; older compliant 100 W / 5 A products still exist.

SPR and EPR are not product nicknames

Standard Power Range stays at 20 V or below. Extended Power Range adds higher voltage profiles up to 48 V and an overall 240 W ceiling. A dock advertising a large power supply does not automatically pass EPR to the host.

A safe decision sequence

  1. Find the device’s documented voltage/current profile or PPS range.
  2. Check one charger port under the exact occupied-port combination.
  3. Calculate required current and select the cable class.
  4. Verify PPS, EPR, or vendor-specific requirements separately.
  5. Treat the result as declared fit until negotiation is measured.
