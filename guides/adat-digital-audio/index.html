ADAT & digital audio guide

An optical connection has more than one job.

Separate format, one-way channel budget, clock reference and audio routing. Solving one layer does not prove the others.

1. Read the format and direction

“Optical” describes a physical path, not necessarily the digital audio format. TOSLINK is used for both ADAT and optical S/PDIF. Sending one into an input set for the other is not solved by another passive cable. Read the active port mode and connect OUT to IN. A switchable optical port may expose a different set of inputs after its mode changes.

Use the Optical Audio Format Checker for a declared pair. It does not select a TV DAC, decode Dolby/DTS or guarantee that a converter supports the required direction/rate. A device that changes connector/transport is not automatically an ADAT/S/PDIF format translator.

2. Count channels at the intended audio rate

The last row is conditional arithmetic, not a statement that your interface has those ports or modes. A unit may support 192 kHz analogue operation while its optical expansion stops at a lower rate. Check its exact manual and firmware.

Example: two expander OUTs into one receiver IN at 96 kHz gives one optical link’s four slots, even if both boxes advertise eight analogue inputs. Buying a second cable cannot add the missing receiving port. Two real IN ports still do not override a receiver’s smaller per-rate channel ceiling. Enter both ceilings in the ADAT Expansion Planner; zero means unknown, not unlimited. Its bundle worksheet is logical, not a universal DAW channel-number map.

3. Draw timing separately from audio

In one synchronous two-device domain, choose a supported timing leader and a follower input that actually receives that leader’s reference. With only expander B OUT → interface A IN, embedded timing travels B → A. If A must remain leader, B needs another supported timing path from A. Depending on the devices this could be a return ADAT connection or a documented Word Clock OUT → IN connection.

A dedicated word-clock cable supplies timing only; it is not the missing audio link. Not every digital receiver can follow its optical input. Apogee Quartet’s fixed-leader rule is a useful counterexample to universal setup recipes. The Clock Route Planner preserves capability and rate unknowns instead of choosing a leader from a brand name.

BNC base/double/quad-rate modes and termination are exact-model matters. The selected audio sample rate is not a universal prescription for BNC clock frequency. Multi-unit distribution, passive T chains and asynchronous sample-rate conversion are outside this worksheet. Check current manufacturer firmware notes as well as the manual.

4. If it locks but stays silent, follow one channel

  1. Observe a named source input meter.
  2. Confirm that channel is routed to the intended digital output.
  3. Observe the matching receiver digital-input meter.
  4. Observe the corresponding DAW input, not a local analogue or playback meter.
  5. Only then inspect its monitor/output destination at a low normal listening level.

A lock indicator is timing evidence, not proof of these routes. Clicks can be intermittent even if one snapshot says locked. Missing channels after a rate change can be a port-map or capacity change rather than a failed cable. The Digital Audio Failure Isolator keeps unobserved points unknown and ranks a next test.

Compare one documented known-good external cable with modes, ports and clock unchanged. If the outcome changes, the original path is implicated; reseating may also matter. Do not reset all software, modify electrical wiring or look into optical emitters to follow this guide. Use equipment status indicators and manufacturer-approved external checks.

What these tools deliberately do not claim

No audio permission, hardware scan, exact product compatibility list, negotiated-rate readout or prediction of jitter/sound quality is provided. The numerical budget is conditional on your declarations. Independent USB audio-driver aggregation, networked audio and multichannel TV decoding are different problems.

Primary evidence and next steps

The rules are grounded in Audient’s dual-optical example, PreSonus S/MUX, Focusrite clock-source guidance and standalone routing documentation. The source Reference records scope and exceptions instead of extending one model’s behavior to all devices.
